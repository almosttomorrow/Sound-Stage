How it works, and why it can’t use Spotify

Every room is real. Each one has real measurements — how big it is, what the walls are made of, where the speakers hang and where you’re standing. The app works out how sound bounces around that space and how it reaches each of your ears a fraction differently. That difference is what makes it feel like you’re in the room rather than listening to an effect.

Every room is the best version of itself. Not an average stadium with a tired PA — a properly tuned system in a room that’s been looked after. The size, the distance and the reverb are all honest. The gear is as good as it gets.

You’re always in the best spot. The sweet spot in a studio, the middle of the crowd in a stadium. Nothing to fiddle with.

One small cheat. Sound takes a fifth of a second to cross a stadium. We skip that wait so the music starts the moment you press play.

Why not Spotify? Spotify and Apple Music lock their audio so no other app can touch it. That’s true on every phone, so this app has to play the music itself. On a computer you can also share a browser tab, as long as it isn’t a locked-down player.

If it stutters: Shorter trims the reverb tail and is easier on older phones.

Use headphones. On speakers you’d be hearing a room inside a room.
