What this is actually doing, and why Spotify can't be piped through it

The model. Each room is a real shoebox in metres with real surface materials. The app traces the direct sound and every early reflection off those surfaces with an image-source model, in seven octave bands, then continues the decay with a diffuse tail whose reverberation time comes out of the Eyring equation for that room. Each reflection is placed at both of your ears separately, with the timing and the frequency-dependent shadowing a head of that size would cause. That is what makes the room appear outside your head rather than between your ears. The loudspeaker voicing is applied before the room, in the order reality does it.

The source. Nothing running on a phone can read the audio coming out of another app. On iOS there is no system-audio capture at all, and the streaming services encrypt their playback on every platform. So this app has to be the player. Files work everywhere; on a desktop browser you can also share a tab and process whatever it is playing, as long as that tab is not using DRM.

Every room is the best version of itself. Not an average stadium with a rung-out PA — a properly flown array that a system engineer has actually tuned, in a room with the treatment a good example of it would have. The distant rigs even get the high end shelved back up to replace what ninety metres of air takes out, which is exactly what someone does at front of house. What stays honest is the physics: the size, the distance, the decay. A stadium is still a stadium.

Where you stand is part of the room. There is an obvious place to be in each of these rooms — the sweet spot in a control room, the middle of the bowl in a stadium — so the app puts you there rather than asking. How hard each system is driven is set the same way: a club rig is wide open, a pair of studio monitors is not.

One thing is deliberately not real. Sound takes two tenths of a second to cross a stadium, and the model knows it — but nobody wants to wait through that every time they press play, so the flight time is trimmed off the front. The far smaller gap between the two speakers is kept, because that is what places the stereo image.

If it stutters. Full length uses the whole measured decay. Light halves it, which builds a room about twice as fast on a slower phone.

Headphones, please. The whole point is that you supply the ears. Played on speakers you would be listening to a room inside a room.
